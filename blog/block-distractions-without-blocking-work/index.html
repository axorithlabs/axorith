Website blocking

Block distracting sites without breaking your work

A useful website blocklist is selective enough to leave your actual research and work intact.

Download for WindowsGitHub

The bluntest website blocker can also be the least useful: a broad restriction may hide the online course, documentation or reference page you need to finish the task. Better blocking starts with a specific workflow, a small number of repeat distractions and a test before the session becomes restrictive.

Separate distraction from legitimate research

During programming, documentation, technical forums and occasional videos may be part of the work. During study, course pages and lectures can be essential. List your actual required resources first, then choose which sites or categories are irrelevant to that session. The same domain can be useful in one context and distracting in another.

Attach the rules to the right preset

Axorith Site Blocker can apply selected site or category rules while the preset is active. Your Programming, Study and Relax presets do not need identical restrictions. This prevents an all-day global blacklist from affecting unrelated contexts when you have finished the focus session.

Verify the setup before a long block

Open the course, project documentation, sign-in page and other services that you rely on. Check blocking in the browsers you actually use. A short trial reveals missing exceptions before a longer or more committed session. If something important fails, refine the rule rather than assuming the user should work around it.

Measure usefulness by completed work

A perfect count of blocked domains does not tell you whether the setup helped. Did you start promptly? Could you reach necessary information? Did the block stop the frequent detour? Keep the rules that reduce avoidable switching and remove the ones that interfere with the real task.
