Focus sessions

Choose the right commitment level for a focus session

More restrictive is not always more productive. Match the session rules to the work and the risk.

Download for WindowsGitHub

Focus tools often advertise their hardest blocking mode. But a short writing session, a software build and a timed exam preparation session have different requirements. Axorith places Focus Commitment alongside launchers, blockers and stop conditions, so the level of restriction can fit the workspace rather than apply to every session.

Start with a flexible routine

For exploratory work, use a session that you can stop and adjust without difficulty. Keep essential applications reachable, test website rules and verify that you can complete normal tasks. The first question is whether the preset improves your routine, not how difficult it is to leave.

Use stronger commitment selectively

A more restrictive mode can make sense for a predictable block of writing, reading or practice. First confirm the allowed applications, breaks and end conditions. Do not use the strictest setting just to compensate for an incomplete setup: an accidentally blocked tool becomes more frustrating when you cannot exit normally.

Define the end before the start

Axorith supports session rules such as a fixed duration or stop time, with optional handoff to another preset. Pick the rule that matches the task. A time limit works for a contained focus period; a fixed stop time can fit a recurring work schedule. Neither replaces your judgment about unfinished critical tasks.

Account for interruptions

A useful preset should respect urgent calls, accessibility needs and recovery steps. Test a short session before extending it. The objective is to reduce unnecessary exits, not to make the computer unsafe or unusable when circumstances change.
