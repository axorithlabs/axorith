Study workflow

Set up a distraction-limited study session on Windows

Keep the tools for the assignment open while reducing access to the things that pull you away.

Download for WindowsGitHub

A study session often needs a PDF reader, notes, a browser and sometimes a calculator or video lecture. Blocking the whole internet is rarely a good starting point. A useful setup separates study resources from unrelated browsing and gives the session a reasonable time boundary.

Describe the actual assignment environment

Create a Study preset and add the notes application, reference material and browser you need. If the assignment uses a university website, keep that site accessible. Preparing the exact tools is usually more valuable than opening a generic “study mode” with no materials ready.

Block the obvious detours, not the course

Use Site Blocker for unrelated social, shopping or entertainment browsing, and App Blocker for desktop distractions. Check whether your course depends on a service that shares a broad category with blocked sites. Narrowing the blocked list is better than discovering halfway through a lecture that the required page cannot be opened.

Make the session small enough to finish

Choose a duration that fits the assignment, then decide whether you need breaks. Focus Commitment belongs to the preset, but stronger restrictions are optional. A short session that preserves access to learning material is usually a better starting point than a long locked session you have not tested.

Review what interrupted the work

After the session, note whether the obstacle was a blocked resource, missing document or unrelated app. Fix the preset rather than adding more restrictions by default. The aim is to spend less effort preparing to study and more effort on the material itself.
